# Senegal JETP: turning an energy strategy into financeable projects

Research note, 13 September 2026. Complements the [43-project documentary
review](jetp-senegal-review-2026-09-13.md). New public sources are archived in
the JETP corpus; `sources.csv` and `manifest.csv` provide URLs, retrieval
outcomes and content hashes. Interpretations below are distinguished from
observations. This is a case argument for further research, not an assessment
of measured programme impact.

The later [managed research rounds](jetp-senegal-managed-rounds-2026-09-13.md)
add procurement and contract records while preserving the coverage counts and
the distinction between contextual finance and attributed project finance.

The strongest finding is the gap between three objects often described with
the same word, “project”: an investment-plan proposal, a site undergoing
preparation, and a financed legal entity. Senegal makes that gap unusually
visible. A proposed national-ownership model can share locations and capacities
with a utility-led development without evidence that its ownership structure
survived. Studies and memoranda show activity, while leaving the financing
claim unresolved. Counting either document as implementation of the whole
investment proposal would erase the very transformation the case should explain.

The current ledger still contains 43 project/programme identities from 49 plan
lines, with 20 direct corroborations and 23 unresolved direct identities.
These counts measure documentary coverage. They are neither counts of power
plants nor a success rate for investment. None of the new documents establishes
a project-level disbursement.

The political declaration of 22 June 2023 envisaged EUR2.5bn of new and
additional financing over an initial three-to-five-year period from 2023. It
required an investment plan within twelve months, explicitly leaving that
process to clarify which projects Senegal and its partners would finance.
The pledge was therefore not a list of approved transactions.
[Political declaration, paragraphs 12–13](https://international-partnerships.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-06/political-declaration-for-a-jetp-with-senegal_fr.pdf).

## A financial design for national ownership

The investment plan gives a concrete mechanism behind its objective of
Senegalese participation. QW2, “Champions Nationaux”, would combine Fonsis,
Senelec and Senegalese private shareholders in an independent power producer.
QW3, “Leral”, envisages Senelec, Senegalese private investors and a Leral fund.
The text presents these shareholders as possibilities, not a constituted
shareholding register. Each vehicle would own 100MW of PV with 60MW/180MWh
of storage. Main plan, printed pp.53–54, source
`sen-investment-plan-l4-mirror`.

| Proposed resources, each vehicle | EUR million | Share of EUR215.9m package |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shareholder equity | 3.63 | 1.68% |
| Concessional mezzanine debt | 32.65 | 15.12% |
| Concessional senior debt | 172.72 | 80.00% |
| Technical assistance | 5.00 | 2.32% |
| Grant | 1.90 | 0.88% |
| Total | 215.90 | 100.00% |

Percentages are calculations from the plan, rounded to two decimals. Debt is
EUR205.37m, or 95.12% of total resources. The plan explicitly seeks maximum
leverage and concessional JETP terms to lower the entry cost for Senegalese
shareholders. Its footnote describes mezzanine debt as financing most of the
junior layer. A headline about “equity mobilisation” therefore needs to distinguish
shareholders' cash from borrowed junior capital. Public Senegalese ownership
also cannot automatically be classified as private mobilisation.

This is an analytically stronger proposition than simply saying that the JETP
has too much debt. The design assigns concessional lending the task of producing
national ownership with a small cash contribution. Whether it can also deliver
low tariffs depends on the eventual lending terms, payment arrangements and
risk allocation, none of which this model table settles. It is a financing
hypothesis. The EUR215.9m totals remain needs in the ledger, and the two vehicles
must not be collapsed because their financial templates are identical.

Papa Daouda Diene and his NRGI co-authors likewise question the plan's thin
capitalisation, grant provision and implementation sequencing. Their January
2026 briefing is a useful contemporary intervention by analysts into financial
design, rather than evidence of funds already paid. Its projected concentration
of payments in 2026–2027 must retain that prospective status.
[NRGI, 26 January 2026](https://resourcegovernance.org/publications/faire-du-plan-dinvestissement-jetp-du-senegal-un-cadre-realisable-et-equitable).

## Solar sites reveal the missing institutional join

| Plan identity | Newly located evidence | Interpretation retained |
|---|---|---|
| QW1 / Annex submission 15, two 50MW plants | ARTELIA's biodiversity metadata names Touba-Toul and Déaly, with Senelec and AFD | Corroborates this preparation package; no finance terms supplied |
| QW2, Champions Nationaux | Plan p.53 names Koungheul and Linguère; Ouarkhokh ESIA and Senelec/Huawei announcement concern corresponding solar development | Strong follow-up route, but ownership/vehicle continuity is unconfirmed |
| QW3, Leral | Proposed new 100MW, plan p.54 | No attributable later project vehicle identified |

ARTELIA's deposit describes two 50MWc plants, each with 30MW/90MWh storage,
at Touba-Toul in Thiès region and Déaly in Louga region. It refines the plan's
“Thiès & Touba” shorthand; Touba-Toul should not silently become Touba city.
The GBIF record has publication metadata of 11 March 2026 and registration on
27 March. Neither date is a construction date. The funding field names AFD
but gives no amount, instrument or agreement date.
[ARTELIA, DOI 10.15468/r5fqf9](https://www.gbif.org/dataset/081fc123-1a65-4d0f-a9a0-a53c939d6727/project).

The provisional Ouarkhokh ESIA is dated February 2026. Its p.16 describes a
Senelec/Huawei memorandum for 50MWc plus 30MW/90MWh in Linguère and invokes
BOAD's environmental requirements. That establishes a development and study
context, not BOAD financing approval. The report already refers to a memorandum
before the August news: the later announcement cannot establish the first-ever
agreement date. [BOAD-hosted ESIA, p.16](https://admin.boad.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rapport_EIES-Centrale-solaire-Ouarkhokh_Send-Senelec_Huwaei.pdf).

A ministry report says that Senelec and CNTIC signed two EPC+F contracts on
28 July 2025, in partnership with Huawei, for two 50MWc plants. It describes
storage as 30MW/90MWh without resolving whether that quantity is aggregate or
per plant. The named contract parties and Huawei's partner role should not be
collapsed. [Ministry, 28 July 2025](https://energie-mines.gouv.sn/partenariat-senelec-et-huawei/).

The ministry subsequently reported the Linguère foundation-stone ceremony in
an article published on 10 April 2026, while Genertec's 26 May account names
Senelec as project owner and says the plant entered implementation. Publication
dates do not establish the exact ceremony day. BOAD's 27 March communiqué adds
a distinct financial stage: under its heading for approved loans for partial
project financing, it reports FCFA41.5bn for Senelec's 50MWc plus 30MW/90MWh
Linguère plant. This is an approval, not a signed agreement, effectiveness or
disbursement observation. [Genertec, 26 May 2026](https://www.intlgt.cn/xwzx/gsxw/202605/t20260526_57364.html);
[BOAD, printed pp.2–3](https://admin.boad.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/COMPRESS-CA-150-1.pdf).

Senelec's own public post announces two 50MW plants at Linguère and Koungheul
and an agreement in Shenzhen. RTS dates the ministerial visit to 11 August 2026.
The announcement does not identify the Champions Nationaux SPV or its
shareholders. Matching places and capacity warrants a provisional link,
not a declaration that the plan's proposed ownership model was implemented.
[Senelec announcement](https://fr.linkedin.com/posts/senelecofficielle_senelec-energy-senegal-activity-7493684231382962176-uODO),
[RTS, 12 August 2026](https://www.rts.sn/actualite/detail/a-la-une/linguere-et-koungheul-le-senegal-et-huawei-scellent-un-accord-pour-deux-centrales-solaires-de-50-mw).

The unresolved historical question is precise: did the nationally owned IPP
proposal evolve into these developments, or are they separate schemes using
similar locations? A project identifier, procurement package and shareholding
record would decide. The ledger preserves the plan identity pending that join.

## Rural preparation is visible outside the agency portal

MRV's own catalogue lists a 2025 Senegal feasibility study for 10MW of mini-grids
in 350 villages. This is substantially more specific than a general company
capability statement. It is nonetheless a study reference, without the village
list or an ASER contract identifier in the collected page. Its link to Annex
submission 5 remains provisional, and the blocked ASER route stays recorded.
[MRV project catalogue](https://www.mrvenergy.com/items?lang=en).

A public LinkedIn repost reproduces MRV's description of GFA/GIZ PED II support
and explicitly associates the 350-village work with ASER. This strengthens the
lead but does not replace the missing contract or study deliverable. Bakel and
Rao Peulh are also mentioned in that post; their capacities must not be added
to the 350-village scheme. [Contractor announcement reproduced on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jeremy-voisin-6a495b26_senegal-solarenergy-batterystorage-activity-7351245157989605376-d4qH).

The practical implication is that agency website failure is an observation
about access, not implementation. Consultancy portfolios and study archives
can expose preparation activity, but do not establish commissioned connections.

ARCOP's ASER audit supplies an exact contract-level join for the separate
PUELEC parent programme. Tender `T_ASER_003` produced contracts `T0038` and
`T0039` with Global Engineering SARL; the two values sum exactly to the
FCFA1,429,995,123 package later reported by the ministry. The audit documents
signed contracts, start orders and issued payment orders for a 20% advance.
It does not prove bank settlement, completion of the wider programme or any
JETP/IPG attribution. It also contains no crosswalk to the distinct 350-, 330-
or 600-village submissions. [ARCOP audit, printed pp.55–56](https://arcop.sn/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/RAPPORT-AGENCE-SENEGALAISE-DELECTRIFICATION-RURALE-ASER-2023-1.pdf).

For public-building solar autonomy, an ANER 2025 general procurement notice
recovered from a historical web archive plans consultant recruitment for
feasibility studies. This preserves a preparation route for Annex 23 but names
no sites, award, budget, universities or hospitals. It cannot make the separate
192-health-facility D2B operation coextensive with the broad submission.
[ANER AGPM 2025, archived route](https://www.aner.sn/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/AGPM-2025.pdf).

Senelec's 2026 procurement plan similarly lists a consultant for a mobility-
electrification master plan concerning charging infrastructure, funded in the
row by an ACF grant. The source is a strong Annex 35 preparation candidate,
but no exact project identifier, award, completed study or deployed charger
supports promotion beyond `central_only`.
[Senelec PPM 2026, p.13](https://www.senelec.sn/media/marches/documents/PPM_SENELEC_2026_VERSION_2_VALIDE_LE_13_03_2026.pdf).

## The renewable target coexists with gas expansion

The government's sector policy explicitly combines domestic gas with renewable
energy. On p.56 it envisages 1,100MW of gas turbines over 2024–2034 alongside a
36.1% renewable share in 2029 and the JETP's 40% in 2030. It also gives Senelec
a separate objective of 40% of total installed capacity: those two 40% figures
refer to different things. Page 57 prioritises rural electrification and gives
2023 urban/rural access rates of 98.2%/65.6%.
[MEPM, sector policy 2025–2029, pp.56–57](https://energie-mines.gouv.sn/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/LPDSEM-2025-2029-_VF_Print.pdf).

Gas was present in the founding bargain, rather than appearing only in the
later sector policy. Paragraph 7 of the 2023 declaration recognises domestic
gas as a transition fuel replacing heavy fuel oil and describes emissions
reductions relative to the 2020 NDC's business-as-usual scenario. A reduction
against that counterfactual is a different claim from a fall in absolute
emissions. [Political declaration, paragraph 7](https://international-partnerships.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-06/political-declaration-for-a-jetp-with-senegal_fr.pdf).

The analytical implication is a measurement boundary. A rising renewable
share of installed MW does not determine the renewable share of generated
MWh, the utilisation of gas plants or absolute emissions. Demand growth,
dispatch and retirements remain necessary evidence. Nor does the coexistence
of gas and renewables prove that JETP loans finance gas infrastructure: that
requires tracing the actual use of funds. Keep energy-system dependence
separate from eligible expenditure and attributed emissions benefits.

## What “just” requires observing

Ouarkhokh's consultation annex records a 10 December 2025 meeting with the
Linguère forestry service. Its concerns include clearing a 100ha site, woodland
and biodiversity loss, and effects on pastoral activities. These are recorded
concerns about anticipated impacts, not verified damage or compensation paid.
They make land use and livelihoods observable questions alongside MW and euros.
[ESIA, printed pp.346–347](https://admin.boad.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rapport_EIES-Centrale-solaire-Ouarkhokh_Send-Senelec_Huwaei.pdf).

NRGI's May 2026 territorial briefing stresses local participation, durable jobs
and benefits for women and young people. It is useful evidence of how civil
society formulates justice claims. Its introductory text also calls 11 projects
financed and the plan unpublished; neither statement is adopted into this
ledger. The first needs financing documents and the second conflicts with the
retrieved public plan. Its site/technology labels likewise need checking before
reuse. [NRGI, 25 May 2026](https://resourcegovernance.org/fr/publications/voix-des-territoires-experiences-et-defis-de-la-transition-energetique-au-senegal).

A defensible case therefore follows the distribution of land costs, electricity
service, ownership and income, rather than treating “just” as a label inherited
from a funding programme. This is a research proposition, not a finding that
the new projects have already failed those tests.

## Mobilised money remains an unresolved quantity

Senego reports on 12 August 2026 that less than EUR1bn had been “mobilised”,
attributing the statement to transition director Yaye Catherine Diop through
Dakaractu. The original accounting table, instrument breakdown and definition
were not obtained. It is a dated, attributed claim, not a usable disbursement
total. No percentage of the EUR2.5bn pledge is computed from it.
[Senego, 12 August 2026](https://senego.com/senegal-le-partenariat-jetp-a-mobilise-moins-dun-milliard-deuros-en-3-ans_1992808.html).

The earlier review's Diass groundbreaking and IFC BioGNV advisory agreement
supply more specific observations than this aggregate. They establish different
stages for different interventions. An engineering study, a memorandum,
a financing approval, an executed loan and a payment should remain separate
rows even when public communications group them under “mobilisation”.

## Evidence that would change the case

1. **Solar ownership and contracts:** obtain the QW2 SPV/shareholding and
   procurement identifiers; reconcile the July CNTIC contracts with the
   February ESIA and August Huawei memorandum.
   This would decide whether the national-ownership proposal persisted.
2. **Actual financial flows:** obtain signed agreements, amounts paid and
   instrument-level reporting behind the mobilisation claim. Separate grant,
   advisory, sovereign loan and project debt, with dates and attribution rules.
3. **Rural perimeter:** obtain the MRV/GFA study identifier and village list,
   then reconcile it with the ASER submission and other rural programmes.
4. **Distribution and operation:** follow consultation commitments through land
   decisions and compensation, and distinguish new connections from service
   quality. For decarbonisation, collect generation and dispatch alongside MW.

This evidence could confirm, modify or reject the working interpretation:
Senegal's JETP is a process of constructing financeable assets and ownership
arrangements within an expanding gas-and-renewables system. The surviving
public documents show that construction process more clearly than they show
the final flow of money or distribution of benefits.

The [managed rounds](jetp-senegal-managed-rounds-2026-09-13.md) then identified its contracts, recovered preparatory procurement records and located BOAD's Linguère approval without resolving the Champions Nationaux ownership crosswalk or adding JETP-attributed finance.
