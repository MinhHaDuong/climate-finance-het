# Astra and Fable: competing proposals for a causal JETP paper

14 September 2026. Phase: Imagine. **There is no selected design.** The author
requested both models to develop options, then authorised supplying Fable with
the full relevant local project context for this discussion.

Astra worked independently from the tickets, programme and evidence audit.
Fable 5.1 ran through the Claude CLI with the same core context and read-only web
research. Neither received the other's first report. After both completed, Astra
critiqued Fable's claims and Fable responded to the coordinator's specific
methodological questions. The original reports and Fable's corrections are retained:

- [Astra's independent proposals](jetp-causal-design-astra-2026-09-14.md).
- [Fable's independent proposals and reconsideration](jetp-causal-design-fable-2026-09-14.md).

These are research proposals, not verified empirical findings. Institutional
leads in the reports still require documentary checking. Bibliographic/source
access limitations are stated in the reports; model agreement does not validate
an identification assumption.

## Options worth comparing

| Proposal | Question it could answer | Evidence it needs | Central vulnerability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-existing pipeline with contemporary country comparators | Did JETP exposure advance the next milestone for operations already pending before intervention? | Historical pipeline membership, maturity, dated outcomes, comparable earlier risk sets and contemporary controls | Nonrandom country selection into an impending energy-specific change; narrow incumbent-pipeline estimand |
| Country-level pipeline throughput | Did JETP increase milestones attained or projects progressing in the country? | Stable country outcome series and definitions; pre-treatment baseline and contemporary comparisons | Combines project creation, composition and progression; cannot be called a waiting-time effect |
| Energy versus non-energy triple difference | Did energy progress change relative to other sectors more in JETP countries? | Comparable sector outcomes and historical sector-gap trajectories in both country groups | Sector-specific concurrent shocks; non-energy exposure makes the effect relative rather than total |
| Country-specific synthetic control | Did a country's trajectory depart from a credible weighted untreated history? | An informative pre-period series, admissible donors, stable measurement and predictive support | Sparse outcomes, donor contamination or a country-specific shock; good fit alone is insufficient |
| A genuine support-allocation mechanism | Did a particular offer of assistance affect eligible projects, possibly near a threshold? | Actual rules, candidate lists and implementation evidence for a cutoff, lottery or exogenous rollout | No mechanism has been established; any effect may be local to one component |
| Project-support comparison | Did a dated offer of support affect projects relative to otherwise comparable unsupported operations? | Admission dates, candidate/rejected histories, baseline maturity and credible confounding controls | Selective admission and within-country spillovers; observed balance cannot rule out hidden selection |

Countries considered for JETP but not concluding an agreement are a potentially
informative **comparator-discovery route**, not another identification mechanism.
Their reasons for non-conclusion may directly concern energy trajectories, and
negotiations or parallel transition programmes may already expose them to support.
Lender contrasts and withdrawal shocks are additional leads from Fable, requiring
a separate intervention definition and evidence before they become designs.

## What cross-review changed

Fable explicitly withdrew or qualified several claims after Astra's critique:

1. Few treated countries do not make permutation inference mandatory or exact.
   Any reference distribution needs a stated justification; placebo ranks alone
   do not recreate random assignment of politically selected countries.
2. Selecting cohorts by approvals occurring after JETP can condition on a
   treatment-induced milestone. Competing-risk methods do not solve this selection.
3. Maturity measured before exposure may be an appropriate baseline covariate.
   Composition or instrument changes induced by treatment are different objects.
4. A negotiation funnel improves the substantive argument for comparability; it
   does not identify assignment. Synthetic control is a distinct proposal, not
   merely a display of difference-in-differences.
5. Eight pre-periods and 25–40 comparator countries were speculative targets,
   not justified acceptance thresholds. Low power differs from failed identification.

Fable also corrected its pre-period chronology and its blanket description of
clock-resetting as immortal-time bias. Exposure classification and the allocation
of follow-up must be examined before naming that specific bias.

**An important issue remains unresolved.** Fable's revised rolling-landmark panel
is useful for reconstructing earlier comparable risk sets, but re-forming risk
sets after policy onset can still condition on treatment-altered membership.
For an incumbent-pipeline effect, freeze the relevant population before exposure
and follow it. For changing post-onset populations, explicitly define a dynamic
or throughput estimand and justify its identification. Merely changing row dates
from approval to landmark dates does not settle the issue.

The two reports favour starting with contemporary country comparisons, but this
is a provisional acquisition preference. No data audit has yet established the
required risk sets, outcome histories, counterfactual assumptions or uncertainty
procedure. Nor can different uncertainty methods simply be combined without
checking that their assumptions apply jointly to the chosen estimator.

## Proposed next discussion and evidence exercise

Choose what we most want the eventual paper to explain: acceleration of a pipeline
already waiting for finance, expansion/progression of the whole pipeline, or the
relative priority given to energy compared with other sectors. These can yield
different effects even when measured perfectly.

Before choosing the design, a bounded evidence exercise can serve several options:

- Reconstruct one lender's historical energy and administratively comparable
  non-energy pipeline in a JETP country and candidate comparison countries,
  including stalled/cancelled operations and earlier landmark populations.
- In parallel, reconstruct the country-selection and negotiation timeline and
  any actual project-support allocation rules, recording unavailable evidence.
- Audit milestone and observation dates, baseline maturity, sector membership,
  and exposure to other reforms; evaluate whether the data permit each proposed
  population and outcome rather than merely more observations.

This refines ticket 0729's existing feasibility mission. Selection of an estimator,
primary outcome and protocol remains a subsequent decision. The causal ambition
is retained; no design is ratified by this comparison note.
