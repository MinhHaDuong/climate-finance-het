# Competing causal designs for JETP preparation and financing

Independent Astra assessment, 14 September 2026. Phase: Imagine. This is a portfolio of research proposals, not approval of a design. No effect has been estimated. I read the three current tickets (0729, 0730, and 0732), the programme conception note, the progress audit, and applicable repository instructions. I did not read another agent's assessment. Institutional and methods sources inspected online are linked beside the relevant claims.

The strongest immediate investment is reconstructing pre-existing lender pipelines and their historical counterparts while searching separately for an actual allocation mechanism. My first candidate would estimate partnership exposure for projects already awaiting a specified milestone before intervention. Triple differences and synthetic control remain competing proposals. Neither becomes credible simply because more comparator projects are available.

The main difficulty is substantive: countries may receive a JETP when their energy pipeline is about to change for other reasons. Project matching removes neither that selection nor selective admission of nearly finished projects. A short causal paper is possible only if documentary and longitudinal evidence support a particular counterfactual. Current evidence establishes no such counterfactual.

**The questions differ.** A partnership effect covers every baseline-eligible energy operation, including those never labelled JETP. A project-support effect concerns a dated offer of a particular service or financial facility to a defined candidate population. A country-sector contrast can estimate an energy effect relative to a non-energy effect. These are distinct interventions and estimands; success on one does not establish the others.

## The design portfolio

The ranking below concerns the next feasibility investment. It does not rank demonstrated validity: none of these designs has passed the necessary evidence audit. Credibility would rank a verified allocation discontinuity above the observational country designs, but its existence is currently unknown.

| Acquisition priority | Candidate | Causal question | Evidence that could make it competitive | Main reason to abandon or narrow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contemporary country comparison with repeated historical pipeline cohorts | Did partnership exposure advance financing of a pipeline already present before intervention? | Comparable lender histories; observable baseline risk sets; informative pre-period cohort outcomes; a defensible account of selection and concurrent reforms | Pipeline membership or maturity cannot be recovered, or an energy reform coincides with JETP and cannot be separated |
| 2 | Triple difference using non-energy operations | Did JETP change energy-stage progress relative to comparable non-energy-stage progress? | Comparable stage definitions across sectors; a stable counterfactual sector gap; evidence about sectoral resource transfers | Non-energy operations are affected or an independent energy-specific shock remains; the estimand may still be relative, but cannot establish absolute acceleration |
| 3 | Country-specific synthetic control, possibly on the sector gap | Did one country's pipeline performance depart from a convincing synthetic history? | A sufficiently long, stable outcome series; several substantively admissible donors; successful pre-period prediction | Sparse milestone counts, unstable composition, poor support, or a country-specific concurrent shock |
| Parallel, bounded search | Actual cutoff, lottery, or externally scheduled allocation of a defined JETP support component | What does an offer of that component cause among eligible applicants, perhaps locally near a cutoff? | Original rules, complete candidate lists, scores and decisions, documented implementation | No allocation mechanism, discretionary exceptions, manipulated scores, or other benefits changing at the same threshold |
| Last resort | Matched project-support comparison with longitudinal adjustment | What is the effect of a dated support offer conditional on observed histories? | Unusually rich records on rejected/deferred applicants, evolving readiness, and decision reasons | Unobserved political priority or readiness drives support; matching cannot remove it |

Numerical probabilities of feasibility would be speculation before the source audit. The first route currently has the clearest documentary acquisition path; the allocation route has the largest potential identification gain if a mechanism is found. These are judgement calls about research effort, not measured chances of publication.

## Candidate 1: exposure of a pipeline fixed before intervention

A concrete narrow proposal is: **among eligible energy financing operations approved but unsigned immediately before the earliest defensible intervention onset, what was the effect of partnership exposure on signature within twelve months?** Approval-to-signature is a candidate to audit, not a selected primary outcome. The same structure could instead follow signed-but-not-effective operations. A preparation version would follow formally recorded proposals awaiting approval, if that denominator is recoverable.

Let R_c be the documented eligible risk set in treated country c before onset g_c. Let Y_i(d,h) indicate attainment of the specified milestone within h months under partnership exposure d. The target is the average of Y_i(1,12) − Y_i(0,12) over R_c, reported first by country. Fix any pooled weighting rule beforehand: equal country weights answer a different question from weights proportional to baseline operations. This is the effect on an incumbent pipeline, including indirect support. It excludes effects on creating new projects and says little about bilateral or private operations absent from the sampling frame.

| Design element | Concrete proposal to test |
|---|---|
| Observation unit | A stable financing operation awaiting one defined milestone; retain parent project IDs to detect correlated tranches and duplicates |
| Treatment assignment | Country partnership exposure; operation-level observations do not create independent assignments |
| Population | All operations satisfying frozen lender, sector, and instrument eligibility rules and in the relevant stage before onset; inclusion independent of eventual JETP attribution |
| Time zero | Earliest date at which the specified partnership intervention could affect decisions, established from negotiation and programme documents; investigate anticipation before adopting the announcement date |
| Outcome | Milestone attained within a fixed supported horizon; twelve months is a feasibility candidate, with six and eighteen months considered before protocol freeze |
| Historical baseline | Earlier risk sets built by the same rule at repeated landmark dates; each historical follow-up window ends before possible anticipation |
| Contemporary controls | Equivalent baseline risk sets in eligible untreated countries at the treated country's calendar date |
| Baseline adjustment | Historical income and financing eligibility; energy-system conditions and previous pipeline performance; lender and planned instrument; technology, elapsed stage duration, and documented readiness before intervention |
| Exclusions | Predetermined sector/instrument rules and documented lack of common support; never exclusion because a project remained unsigned, disappeared, or was cancelled |

The documentary distinction between policy onset and project age matters. Use policy onset as the start of exposure follow-up while retaining the original project-stage clock and elapsed waiting time. There is no credit for work completed before exposure. If anticipation began before the proposed baseline, an earlier risk set is required; subtracting six months mechanically is not a solution.

A hidden selection problem rules out a tempting shortcut. Restricting the analysis to projects approved after JETP conditions on a milestone the partnership may itself change. Even flawless approval and signature dates would then describe a selected post-treatment population. The proposed pre-onset risk set avoids that particular problem, at the cost of a narrower question and potentially very few cases. Similarly, realised concessionality, the chosen lender, or readiness measured after exposure may be mechanisms rather than admissible matching variables.

There is also a trend-testing trap. Every unsigned member of the onset cohort has zero signature attainment before onset by construction. Those flat pre-period indicators are not evidence for parallel trends. To obtain informative history, construct earlier comparable risk sets and measure their subsequent twelve-month attainment. A difference-in-differences analysis would then concern repeated cohorts of eligible operations, with an explicit assumption about stable or adequately standardised cohort composition. It is not an ordinary balanced panel of unchanged projects. Overlapping historical cohorts induce further dependence.

The identifying claim would be that, absent JETP, the treated country's standardised cohort attainment would have changed like that of its contemporary weighted comparators. Document why the energy pipeline that helped attract JETP would not already have generated a different change. Matching can address observed comparability; country fixed effects cannot remove a new country-specific energy reform. Whether an accompanying reform belongs to the intervention package or confounds it must follow a substantive definition fixed before estimation.

For genuinely staggered adoption, compare each adoption cohort with still-untreated or never-treated countries over uncontaminated windows. Callaway and Sant'Anna provide group-time treatment effects and covariate-adjusted identification under conditional parallel trends; their framework does not establish that assumption for JETPs or solve a changing project denominator. [Callaway and Sant'Anna, *Difference-in-Differences with Multiple Time Periods*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09015).

An alternative country-panel outcome is total milestones per quarter relative to a fixed pre-treatment portfolio size. That can include newly generated projects without dividing by a post-treatment risk set. It measures pipeline throughput, combining entry and progression. It cannot be silently described as a reduction in project waiting time. This is a separate proposal if the author values the wider partnership effect more than stage-specific speed.

## Candidate 2: what non-energy controls buy

On an additive attainment-probability scale, the proposed contrast is:

    DDD = (change in energy − change in non-energy) in JETP countries
        − (change in energy − change in non-energy) in comparator countries.

Triple differences need a counterfactual parallel trend for this sector gap. Separate parallel trends for both sectors are not necessary: their country-comparison biases may cancel. That is the reason DDD can help when an economy-wide shock differs between treated and comparison countries. It does not automatically cancel an energy-specific shock. The outcome scale and covariate standardisation matter. [Olden and Møen, *The triple difference estimator*](https://academic.oup.com/ectj/article/25/3/531/6545797).

Applied here, DDD could remove a general improvement in ministries' loan-signing capacity while retaining the energy-specific change. It could also subtract the wrong quantity. If energy gains 0 percentage points and non-energy loses 10 because officials are reassigned to JETP, a DDD of +10 does not show faster energy projects. Conversely, if the partnership improves financial administration equally across sectors, a zero DDD can coexist with useful energy acceleration. Under untreated comparator countries and the sector-gap trend assumption, its natural interpretation is the energy effect minus the non-energy effect. Identifying the energy effect additionally requires a zero non-energy effect or independently supported information about it.

The non-energy proposal deserves acquisition, especially within the same lender and sovereign investment process. Water or transport investment may share more administrative steps with energy investment than emergency health operations or policy loans. This is a hypothesis to check against process documents, not an automatic sector whitelist. Cross-sector matching should use administrative and baseline maturity characteristics; forcing a technology match across sectors would empty the comparison. Mixed energy/transport or climate operations require a predetermined classification rule.

Estimate and display both sector components and the cross-country energy contrast alongside DDD. Investigate shared staff, lender envelopes, fiscal ceilings, and co-financing commitments as possible spillover channels. DDD becomes preferable only if the documents make a common cross-sector confounder more plausible than an energy-specific confounder and support its stated relative or absolute estimand.

## Candidate 3: synthetic control and allocation alternatives

Synthetic control is attractive for transparent country-specific comparisons when there are few treated countries. A country outcome must first exist over a sufficiently informative pre-period. Weights chosen from pre-treatment trajectories can then be checked on reserved pre-treatment periods, with donor exclusions based on substantive exposure and common support. A pooled collection of similar old projects is not this design. Abadie's methods article specifically treats feasibility and data requirements; I inspected its publisher abstract, but full-text retrieval failed in this session. [Abadie, *Using Synthetic Controls*](https://ftp.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.20191450).

My application-specific concern is sparsity: quarterly approval-to-signature rates can swing because one large project enters or leaves a tiny risk set. Longer bins improve stability but shorten the pre-period series. A synthetic country with excellent fit driven by a small number of unusual operations may have little predictive value. A held-out pre-period, donor concentration, and leave-one-donor-out checks would be necessary. A synthetic control on the energy/non-energy gap retains the DDD interpretation problem. Factor models or synthetic difference-in-differences can be additional estimators once a suitable panel exists; changing estimators cannot supply missing identifying evidence.

A real support-allocation rule would change the ranking. An oversubscribed preparation facility might have a score threshold, a lottery, or a documented rollout unrelated to anticipated readiness. No such mechanism has been established in the material reviewed. Seek rules and rejected/deferred candidates before suggesting a regression discontinuity or instrument. A score used only as advice, a queue sorted by maturity, or an arbitrary calendar cutoff does not generate a natural experiment. A valid threshold design would identify an effect near that threshold; it would not estimate the total JETP partnership effect.

A future random allocation of additional preparation assistance could answer a narrower causal question if a programme operator independently chose to cooperate. That is a prospective study with institutional dependencies and later outcomes. It cannot recover the historical effect of the partnerships already announced.

## Observation, falsification, and inference

Stage attainment at a fixed horizon is more defensible than mean duration among successful transitions. Retain cancellation as a competing outcome; treating cancelled operations as ordinary censoring would pretend that they can still attain the milestone through the original operation. Administrative censoring at a common cutoff differs from loss of visibility. Reclassification, replacement finance, and operation splitting need explicit treatment.

Exact event days are not universally necessary. If source bounds establish whether signature preceded the twelve-month horizon, that binary outcome may be known despite an imprecise date. If the event interval straddles the horizon, bound the outcome or use an appropriate interval-censoring model. Publication dates do not establish event times. Unknown event dates are not right-censoring, and a current absence of a document does not establish non-occurrence. Restricted time without attainment is a possible secondary outcome, with cancelled cases retained as unachieved through the horizon; interpret it accordingly.

The design audit should prespecify the following challenges before the primary estimate:

1. Reconstruct historical cohort and, for DDD, sector-gap trajectories. Test whether held-out pre-period outcomes can be predicted; a failure to reject a pre-trend test is weak evidence with sparse data.
2. Place fake interventions sufficiently early that their outcome windows end before plausible anticipation. Keep a record of all windows tried.
3. Check whether documentary availability, reporting delay, sector classification, or lender coverage changes at onset. An apparent event-study effect in disclosure is a warning about the measured outcome.
4. Show energy and non-energy components, country-specific results, baseline maturity strata, and exclusions one donor or treated country at a time. Do not select the specification with the most favourable effect.
5. Map concurrent energy reforms and cross-country financing substitution. Shared donors can transmit JETP effects into comparator countries; exclusion of exposed donors or countries may leave insufficient support.
6. Report sensitivity to plausible departures from the identifying assumption and date bounds. Rambachan and Roth allow restrictions on departures from parallel trends, including bounds calibrated to pre-period deviations. These restrictions remain assumptions and do not cure few-country inference. [Rambachan and Roth, *A More Credible Approach to Parallel Trends*](https://www.jonathandroth.com/assets/files/HonestParallelTrends_Main.pdf).

Four treated countries create at most four country policy assignments, and common negotiations or donors can make them dependent. Neither operation-level standard errors nor resampling individual projects represents that uncertainty. Ordinary country-clustered asymptotics and a wild bootstrap can also misbehave with very few treated clusters. Report country influence and the assumptions behind any inferential procedure. [MacKinnon, Nielsen, and Webb, *Cluster-Robust Inference: A Guide to Empirical Practice*, especially sections 4.3.2 and 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.03285v1).

Country-placebo ranks can be useful diagnostics. They are not exact randomisation p-values when JETP countries were selected politically and comparator assignments are not exchangeable. A model-based interval should identify the model supporting it. Power calculations must preserve country dependence, historical cohort overlap, donor concentration, and observed risk-set sizes. A feasibility memo should state the smallest scientifically useful change the data could resolve, not infer power from 383 project identities. Inability to exclude zero is compatible with an informative causal estimate; inability to identify the counterfactual is a different failure.

## First acquisition exercise and decision rules

The current audit reports 383 named identities, 21 unnamed slots, and 301 sources. Its 451 timing observations include six exact days and two approval-year intervals; 443 have unknown event precision. Those are registry-coding counts, not an audit of everything available in the underlying sources. The 97 closed World Bank operations are discovery leads and cannot define a risk set of all proposals. [Local progress audit](../docs/jetp-programme-progress-2026-09-14.md).

Start with a bounded documentary exercise supporting several designs simultaneously. The existing three-case triad should remain: a mature-on-entry JETP operation, a pending JETP proposal, and an ordinary historical operation. Add a frame test: can the same lender enumerate all relevant operations at an earlier date, including cancellations and proposals that disappeared? Three detailed success stories do not answer that denominator question.

World Bank project profiles expose approval information and associated reports, making them a concrete first discovery route. This confirms a source architecture, not the completeness of historical proposal populations. [World Bank, *Using Projects & Operations*](https://www.worldbank.org/en/archive/digital-collections/using-projects-and-operations). The ADB sovereign dataset supplies another candidate frame covering 2005 onward, with a January 2026 snapshot. Its metadata state that “Active” requires effectiveness for at least one financing source: an active-only selection would therefore condition on a financing outcome. I could inspect the dataset metadata; an individual project-page request returned HTTP 403, so its actual endpoint completeness was not verified. [ADB, *Sovereign Projects*](https://data.adb.org/dataset/adb-sovereign-projects).

Use the first acquisition block to produce a compact evidence matrix:

- For one lender/instrument stratum, enumerate energy and administratively comparable non-energy operations in one JETP country and two plausible comparison countries. Obtain current records and historical evidence for approximately five years before potential onset. These country counts and years are a discovery target, not an adequacy threshold.
- Reconstruct at least three historical landmark risk sets whose follow-up ends before anticipation, plus the actual onset risk set. Record empty cells as evidence about feasibility. Keep candidate comparison countries open until historical income, financing eligibility, and energy conditions are checked.
- Audit the agreed 10–15 named JETP cases for original operation identity, attribution date, earlier maturity, both stage endpoints, source locator, event bounds, reporting lag, and cancellation or loss of visibility. Use a balanced mix of disclosure conditions; this remains an extraction pilot.
- In parallel with source reading, search original partnership and facility documents for admission rules, scored applications, rejected/deferred lists, negotiation onset, and rollout schedules. Record absence of evidence. No contact with institutions or other external communication is authorised by this report.
- Deliver a table with one row per candidate design: available denominator, available historical outcomes, onset ambiguity, overlap, plausible confounders, inference route, and missing evidence. Freeze no primary estimator until this comparison has been reviewed.

NARROW to one lender, country, financing stage, or pre-existing pipeline if that yields a defensible causal question. A narrow sample is preferable to pretending the partnership's entire portfolio was observable. A study of only one support component must name that intervention in its title and causal sentences.

DEFER preparation acceleration if rejected or abandoned proposals cannot be reconstructed well enough to define the approval risk set. DEFER a particular financing-stage proposal if baseline membership and attainment cannot be bounded at a useful horizon. DEFER country-based causal estimation if contemporary support fails, pre-period outcomes cannot be constructed, or a concurrent energy intervention remains observationally inseparable from the JETP exposure being claimed. Lack of an allocation rule closes that route rather than inviting an invented instrument.

Source discovery should stop expanding merely to improve an attractive estimate. After the first lender frame and the 10–15-case audit, decide explicitly which remaining information could change identification; acquire that information in a second bounded block. If none can, return an author scope decision. The observatory and data paper can progress while a historical causal short paper remains unproven.
